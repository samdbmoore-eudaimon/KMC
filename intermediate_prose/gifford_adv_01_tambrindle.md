# gf_tambrindle — "The Vent-Count Watch"

**tagline**
A rush job across the seam, a rival porter's dare, and a vent coming up before he can get home. Tam only has to be quicker than he has ever been.

---

**intro**
Tam heard it through the floorboards before anybody said it out loud. Mother Halloway at the table with the rent docket and the chalk tab from Marta's laid side by side, and no sound at all coming up through the boards, which was the sound she made when there was nothing to be said about it.

Three marks short. Maybe three and a half.

Nobody had told him. Nobody was going to. In that house the money was Rooke's business, the way the pump was Rooke's business and the coal-man's boy was Rooke's business, and Rooke was thirteen and out somewhere being cleverer than everybody, and Tam was eleven months younger and had exactly one thing he was better at.

He was supposed to be mending the barrow wheel. He had been supposed to be mending it for two days. He put the knife away, laced his boots twice each, and went down to the yard to find out what was going spare.

Portering paid by the load and the hour. Today he was going to be the fastest thing on the seam.

---

**speed_check**
Roll-call at the porters' yard shut on second bell and not a breath after, and second bell was already coming up out of the Under somewhere behind him.

Ferrow Lane at that hour was barrows and washing lines and standing water. Tam had run it since he was six. He knew which wall took a vault instead of a corner and which gate stuck if you did not kick the bottom of it, and he knew he had left the house late.

Somebody quick had better make that bell.

---

**speed_miss**
He forgot the gate.

It stuck the way it always stuck, and by the time he had it open the bell had finished and the yard clerk had drawn her line under the list.

"Late's late," she said, without looking up, which was worse than if she had. "There's the leavings. Go and see what nobody wanted."

*(The Weighed Blade stays lost for now. Another run at this morning might turn it up.)*

---

**speed_found**
He took the wall on the vault and came down running, past a jeweller's boy on his knees in the wet with his tray gone over and his hands going after the pieces faster than sense.

One thing was rolling for the drain. Tam put a boot on it, picked it up, and found a boot-knife in his hand: plain handle, no maker's mark, and so evenly weighted it barely registered as being held.

He held it out. The boy said keep it, he had never liked the thing, it would not sit right in any sheath he owned.

It sat right in Tam's. He had his father's knife inside his coat and had never once used it for work and was not going to, which meant he had been portering for two years with nothing on him worth the name.

The Weighed Blade is his now. *(+1 Speed, kept forever.)*

---

**gate_fight** (scene intro)
He made the bell with three heartbeats in hand, which the yard clerk marked down without comment, and then the seam crossing gave him trouble.

The Seam Warden was a big man in a leased grey coat with a lamp on a bracket beside him and a board of dockets at his elbow, and he had the crossing to himself and knew it. The Rise put them on the boundaries to check writs and paid them by the hour, and an hour spent arguing paid the same as an hour spent waving people through.

"Docket."

Tam gave him the docket.

"Out of sequence." The Warden turned it over twice. "State your purpose."

He had a docket. It was not the docket for this crossing at this hour, on account of being early on purpose, and there was no box on the board marked keen.

**The Seam Warden — hitText**
Tam gave him the yard clerk's name and the load number in the same breath and did not leave a gap in it, and the Warden's next objection arrived a half-second after it was any use.

**The Seam Warden — strikeText**
The Warden put an arm across the crossing without hurrying and caught Tam across the shins with the edge of the board, which is a bruise that argues with you all day.

**The Seam Warden — defeatText**
The Warden looked at the docket, and at Tam, and at the queue building behind him, and stood aside without saying anything, because none of them ever say anything.

---

**gate_win**
He went through. Wardens do not apologise. They stop objecting, and in that trade the two things are the same.

At the counter beyond, Coop was waiting. The Weighmaster's clerk: Laachmi, seven feet of him, horns filed blunt as the office required, standing very still the way tall people stand when the room was not built with them in mind.

Coop had a rush job on the board that nobody had taken, on account of what was attached to it, and had looked at Tam and evidently decided he would do.

---

**gate_retreat**
He backed off the crossing before the second one landed, which was sense and took a while to feel like it.

The long way round cost him ten minutes and an elbow off a low wall he misjudged out of temper.

By the time he got to the counter Coop had another name half written on the board, and looked, as far as a Laachmi's face gives anything up, mildly put out at having to cross it back off.

---

**crossroads**
"Valve gaskets." Coop slid a sealed crate and a docket across. "Rise workshop on Founders' Row wants them before the afternoon shift retools. If they stand idle somebody's fee gets docked and it is not going to be mine. Triple rate if it is through their door before third bell. Nothing at all if it is not."

Coop's eyes went to the board and came back.

"Sella's already gone. Down the Scullery Passage. You did not ask and you will want to know it."

Three ways off the yard toward Founders' Row. The Cutthroat Stairs, which were fastest if your knees agreed. The clatterlift queue, which climbed the cliff properly and only stopped at his landing when it felt like it. Or the Scullery Passage, through the back kitchens, the way Sella had gone, if he wanted to find out how fast she actually was.

---

**stairs**
The Cutthroat Stairs went down two hundred and forty feet of cliff in one straight ugly run, and every porter on the seam called them what they were.

At the foot of them sat an outfall grate, and the grate had started to tick.

Tam felt it before he heard it, through the soles of his boots on the stone: the Turn going over somewhere underneath, the knock coming up. The afternoon vent was on its way and it was going to come up through that grate whether he had finished at the bottom of the stairs or not.

He had the crate on his back and about four seconds to decide whether he knew this staircase as well as he thought he did.

---

**stairs_good**
He knew it.

He went down it in three long runs with a hand on the rail and his weight forward, and the grate let out its first breath of white as his boots hit the flat, close enough that his coat came off him at the bottom smelling of hot iron.

Wedged in the drain grille, too corroded for anybody to have bothered with, were the guts of somebody's dropped chronometer. Rusted through and useless as a whole, but the mainspring inside it had been sealed tight enough to come out clean.

He dug it out with the Weighed Blade, wiped it on his sleeve, and pocketed it without knowing yet what it was for.

*Component found: A Tensioned Mainspring.*

---

**stairs_mid**
He got the last flight wrong and took it two at a time on nerve, and the fog came up round his boots on the final landing.

It did not get skin. It got leather, and the leather would carry it for a week.

Whatever was rusting in that grille stayed where it was. Some grates you get one clean pass at, and he had spent his on getting down with both ankles, which was the right way to spend it.

---

**lift**
The clatterlift queue went twice round the winding-house, cars going up the cliff face on their long brass chains.

Every porter on the seam knew the trouble with the clatterlift. One car in three stopped at his landing. The rest went straight past with the chains singing, bound for stops with better connections.

He did not have the time to wait for a certainty. He had the time for one car.

---

**lift_good**
The one that came into the landing was a good one, chain taut, door already swinging. He was in and climbing before the operator had finished calling it.

Somebody had left a dead chronometer wedged in behind the bench seat, years of dust on it, forgotten the way small useless things are forgotten in places nobody stays. He worked the mainspring out of the ruined casing with the cliff sliding past the window and had it away before the car reached the top.

*Component found: A Tensioned Mainspring.*

---

**lift_fail**
It went straight past with the chain singing, bound somewhere he was not going.

He watched it go, and then he went and stood at the back of the queue again, because there was nothing honest to do with his hands and the alternative was thinking about how much of the afternoon that had cost.

The second car stopped. It got him up the cliff. There was nothing behind the seat.

---

**race**
The Scullery Passage ran low and steaming through the back of a dozen Rise kitchens: cooks shouting, pot-boys flattening themselves into the wall, the heat coming off the ranges in sheets.

Forty yards up it, moving as though she had arranged for the passage to be empty, was Sella.

She portered the seam the same as he did, on the same wages and the same worn boots, and had told him more than once and in front of other people that his crate-carrying was an insult to the trade. She had not seen him yet. That was not going to hold.

"Oi," he said, because subtlety had never got him anywhere.

She looked back, took in the crate on his shoulder, and grinned like it was the best thing that had happened to her all week.

---

**race_fight** (scene intro)
"Same crate, same docket, same rate." She did not slow down to say it. "Whoever's foreman signs first gets paid. Whoever's second gets a story about it."

It was not a fair race and it was not meant to be. It was two porters and one passage: corners, cooks' carts, a rack of ladles at head height, and however much of a lead a person can hold through a kitchen with pans on.

**Sella Vane — hitText**
He took the corner half a step tighter than she did and closed it up enough to hear what she thought about that.

**Sella Vane — strikeText**
She went past a cook's cart at exactly the wrong moment for him, and he lost three strides getting out of a rack of ladles.

**Sella Vane — defeatText**
He came out of the last turn a body-length up and did not look back to check, because looking back is how you give it away again.

---

**race_win**
He beat her to the workshop door by less than the length of a barrow, and she came in laughing, which was worse than furious, because now he owed her a rematch and both of them knew it.

"You dropped this back at the third turn." She threw him something small and cold before she had her breath back. "Went straight past a stall selling scrap chronometers and never looked at it. You're welcome."

A mainspring, still coiled tight, worth more to him that afternoon than whatever she could have got for it.

*Component found: A Tensioned Mainspring.*

---

**race_retreat**
She took the last turn wide and mean and left him nowhere to go but wide and slow, and she got to the workshop door far enough ahead that following her in was less a finish than an apology.

"Tell your foreman you were second," she said, up against the doorframe, not unkindly. "Tell him you were quick about it."

It was not nothing. It was not a mainspring either, and she had not found one to spare him.

---

**converge**
Whichever way he had come he came through the door with the crate still up and time still, just, on his side.

The foreman on shift was a broad soot-streaked man called Corbal, who looked at the state of Tam and then at the state of the crate and said, "Sit down before you go over. Docket wants signing first."

Corbal did not sign for anything he had not had the lid off. That was the whole of Corbal.

---

**dock_good**
He had the lid off and his hands in it and came up with nothing to say about it, which from Corbal was the highest available grade of praise. Seals intact, no crush on the corners, not one gasket cut. He signed.

"Scrap bin's there if you want it." He nodded at a crate of broken instrument parts by the door. "Nobody's claimed it in months."

Tam found a small brass count-wheel in the ruin of it, teeth still true, and put it in the pocket with the mainspring, where the two of them were starting to look less like scrap and more like the front end of an idea.

*Component found: A True Count-Wheel.*

---

**dock_mid**
Corbal took the lid off and found a corner gone in and spent some time on it, and in the end decided the damage was the crate's and not the load's, and said so, and signed.

"Good enough."

He did not mention the scrap bin. Tam, by then more or less asleep upright, did not think to ask.

---

**vent_setup**
Docket signed, fee promised, nothing left but the walk home, which was usually the easy part.

The Turn did not care what sort of day anybody had had. He was halfway down off the seam when it went over underneath him, one hard knock up through the boot soles, and every porter on that road knew what came next.

A lane fills in about forty of your own breaths and does not clear again for a good while after. Get it wrong at either end and you are either scalded or standing about in the wet a lot longer than the fee is worth.

---

**vent_check**
The grates along the bottom road were going warm. Not hot. Warm, with that shiver in the ironwork that arrives before the hiss does, and the trick of it is knowing the difference between a grate with time in it and a grate with none.

There is a way to learn that difference and it is expensive.

---

**vent_fail**
He was a breath out.

He got into the shelter of a boarded-up watch-repair kiosk half a second after the white had already taken the cuff of his sleeve, and sat in the dark of it holding his wrist while the lane went blind outside, and there was nothing findable in there in that light.

*(A Sliver of Watch-Crystal stays lost for now. Another run at this stretch of the day might turn it up.)*

---

**vent_good**
He read the grate right, got into the boarded-up kiosk with half a breath in hand, and sat out the whole of it dry and unscalded and extremely bored, going through a tray of parts nobody had cleared out when the place shut.

In among the dead springs and the stripped screws was a single sliver of watch-crystal, thin as a fingernail, and somehow, under all that dust, still perfectly clear.

*Component found: A Sliver of Watch-Crystal.*

---

**forge** (scene intro)
Old Byrne kept a bench at the seam gate mending porters' watches for whatever they had on them, on the grounds that a porter who could not tell the time got scalded eventually and she would rather it was not on her account.

"Parts instead of coin." She looked over what he tipped out: a mainspring, a count-wheel, a sliver of crystal. "That's either clever or broke. Show me all three properly matched and I'll see what a good knife and a steady hand can do about the rest."

**forge — forged**
Byrne turned the pieces over under the lamp, matched the spring to the wheel and the wheel to a scavenged case, and set the crystal over the face with a small click that meant it had seated true first time.

"There." She pushed it across. "That'll keep time honester than any bell on this seam, and not one part of it is answerable to the Rise. Mind the knife you built half of it with stays for whittling and not for anything that bleeds."

The Vent-Count Watch is his! *(+1 Speed, kept forever.)*

**forge — notforged**
Byrne laid out what he had and did not take long over it, because there was not much to take long over.

"Short a piece." She tapped the gap. "A watch wants all three or it's an optimistic paperweight. Keep hold of what you've got. The bench isn't going anywhere and neither is the Turn's schedule, worse luck."

**finale**
He came up Ferrow Lane past dusk with the fee in his pocket, both knees complaining, and something ticking against his ribs that he had not owned that morning.

Rooke was on the step with her back against the frame, doing something to a slate, and looked up at the state of him and worked the whole thing out in about a second and a half, which was how she worked everything out.

She did not say well done. She moved over so he could get past.

Mother Halloway was still at the table with the docket and the chalk tab laid side by side. Tam put the marks down next to them, and nobody made a speech about it, and the two pieces of paper stopped being a problem for the length of a month.

---

**end — goodText**
Marta struck three marks off the tab without a word about it, and the rent went in clean for the first time since the spring.

Sella found him at the yard two days later and wanted her rematch, and got it, and lost it, loudly, in front of witnesses she had brought specially.

He never finished the peg for the barrow wheel.

What he had instead was this. He could stand on any road between the yard and the Under and tell you from the iron under his boots how long he had, and there is no writ in Gifford that gives a man that, and there is nobody in the city who can take it off him at a gate.

---

**end — midText**
Marta took what he had toward the tab and did not press for the rest, which cost her something and which she did not mention. The rent held another week.

That was the job. Nobody had asked it to be more than that, and it was not.

He never finished the peg, and the knife went on finding things to do that were not the peg. But he had learned the shiver of every grate between the yard and the bottom road, and that is not the sort of knowing that goes stale, and it is not the sort anybody checks a docket for.
