# gf_growl — "The Weight of the Round"

**tagline**
Vane wants an example made on Cinder Row today. Growl means to choose who it gets made of.

**intro**
Vane had given Growl the easy half of the job and the boy the hard half, which was Vane's idea of training.

'Take Pitch round with you,' Vane had said, not looking up from the ledger. 'Cinder Row. The Sirrath girl, the one whose father reckoned at Aster Row before his lungs gave out. Three marks owing since spring, and word's got round that Ma Sindry's crew are running chalk cheaper than we are. I want it seen paid, one way or another, and I want it seen by teatime.' A pause, the kind Vane was good at. 'Let the boy do the seeing to.'

Pitch was twelve, or near enough, and had been running errands for the Ashlight for eleven days. He had not yet broken anything that mattered to anyone but a shopkeeper. Growl had watched that particular education happen to other children more times than he cared to count, and had once, a long time ago, been the child it happened to.

'What's she owe it on?' Pitch asked, trotting to keep up.

'Medicine. Her mother's chest.' Growl did not slow down. 'And she's got one thing in that flat worth breaking in front of a street full of witnesses, and it's a clockwork sparrow her father built her before the fog took him. Vane wants it seen. He didn't say what.'

Pitch went a little pale, which was the correct response, and said nothing else the whole way down Tallow Street. Growl let him think.

**finale**
Tansy's door, at last, with the street already gathering the way streets do when they can smell an example coming.

Growl set the axle bearing down on her table with a clang loud enough for the landing to hear, and made a great show of weighing three marks of scrap against three marks of chalk on Vane's borrowed scale, muttering about interest and lateness for the benefit of anyone listening at the door. Underneath the noise, quieter than the scale, he counted out the actual remainder of the debt from his own coat, in mark and cog, and told Tansy to chalk it paid and say nothing about who'd settled it.

The clockwork sparrow sat on the shelf behind her the whole time, wings folded, untouched. Nobody outside that room would ever know it had been in danger at all, which was rather the point.

'That's it?' Pitch asked, once they were back on the stairs. 'That's the lesson?'

'The lesson,' said Growl, 'is that you can make a street believe you've broken something without breaking it. Remember that longer than you remember anything else I teach you.' He didn't say the rest, which was that somebody had once done something similar for a Sirrath boiler-hand a few streets over, steering him toward a workshop instead of a recruitment, and that Growl still didn't know if that had been mercy or just a man placing a bet against himself years too late. Some lessons you pass on before you've finished learning them yourself.

Word got back to Vane, as word always did, that the Cinder Row debt had been seen paid and seen paid hard, and Vane was satisfied in the specific way of a man who only ever checks that the noise was loud enough, never what it was made of.

Tansy's sparrow kept its wings. Pitch walked home a different shape of quiet than he'd walked out in, turning something over that he wasn't ready to say out loud yet and might not be for years. Growl went back to the Ashlight's ledgers that night the same enforcer he'd been that morning, on paper, in the eyes of everyone who mattered to his wages. Not redeemed. Not finished with the job. Just, once again, angry on somebody else's behalf, and quietly certain that particular anger had landed somewhere useful this time.

The debt got paid down further than Vane had any right to expect and not so far as Growl would have liked.
