# gf_marta — "The Quarter-Day Reckoning"

**tagline**
Four marks don't lose themselves. Someone put them there, and Marta means to find out who.

---

**intro**
The chalk-book balanced every quarter-day for eleven years, and then, on a Tuesday that smelled of the Engine's fourth venting, it did not. Marta found the gap by candlelight: four marks unaccounted for in a column she had checked twice already, which meant either her arithmetic had failed her for the first time in her life, or somebody had been busy. She did not believe the first explanation. She rarely did. Outside, the outfall grate on Ferrow Lane coughed its scalding fog into the gutter and the whole street held its breath until the knock came, felt through the soles of every boot at once, the way it always did. Marta shut the book, latched it, and thought carefully about who had been standing near the counter on collection days.

Four marks does not sound like much until you understand that four marks is a family's bread for two days, and that somebody in this street is going without because of it. Marta set her jaw and started at the top of the page.

Whoever had done it either did not understand compounding, or understood it exactly. Marta pocketed the offending page as evidence and, from the drawer beneath the till, took out the brass rule her old master had left her, notched at every half-mark so that nobody, including her, could round in the wrong direction by accident again. She weighed it in her hand and felt steadier for it.

**skillcheck1_fail**
She found nothing wrong in the sums themselves, which unsettled her more than an error would have. It meant the fault was not in her arithmetic but in something the arithmetic couldn't see: a docket that did not match the goods it claimed, or a hand she had not thought to doubt. She rubbed the ache from between her eyes, put the ledger away for the queue's sake, and told herself she would find it before the reckoning.

**afterskill**
The bell above the door coughed, a Rise habit fitted specially, since the Under's own doorbells had all been requisitioned for scrap two winters back, and in came a man Marta had been half expecting since the discrepancy first showed itself: a clerk from Old Custom and Sons, debt-factors of good repute on the Rise and no repute at all on Ferrow Lane. He wore a coat the colour of unpaid rent and carried a folder tied in green ribbon, which in her experience was never a good sign, ribbon being cheaper than honesty and rather more common. 'Notice of inspection,' he said, before she had finished serving the woman in front of him. 'Quarterly reckoning. We hear your book don't balance, Mistress Marta.'

Every street he had ever visited with that folder had ended up owing Old Custom and Sons considerably more than it had started the morning owing anyone. Marta had one quarter-day to prove her book was sound, and Fessup intended to make that as difficult as possible before he left.

**gate_retreat**
She could not spare the time to win the argument outright, not with three customers waiting and the fog due within the hour, so she let him have the last clause and simply agreed to have her book ready by quarter-day. Fessup seemed satisfied with that, which told her rather more about his actual instructions than anything he had read aloud. He left the ribbon's mark pressed into her counter like a bruise. She had a quarter-day now, and a great deal to do in it.

**crossroads**
Four marks does not lose itself. It goes somewhere, or someone puts it somewhere, and either way there was a short list of places to look before quarter-day: Dill Corrigan's account, which had looked odd to her for a month; the delivery register, which the carter filled in by lamplight and the eye of faith; and young Perren, the shop-boy, who had been standing rather too close to the till on collection days for a boy who claimed no interest in numbers.

---

**routeA_setup**
Dill Corrigan wove cloth for half the Under and owed Marta for most of the thread that went into it, paid down slowly on the quarter-day terms she gave anyone steady enough to trust with them: one-eighth off the standing total if it was cleared within the quarter. He came in looking like a man who had already lost an argument with himself. 'Your book says I owe more than I make sense of,' he said, and put his own tally slate on the counter, which was, Marta noted, the single most useful thing any customer had done for her all month.

---

**routeA_good**
225 marks, and not the 240 the ledger under Marta's own hand currently showed. She turned the page round so Dill could watch her work it, and he did, visibly relieved to find the fault was not in his memory. Somebody had recorded his loaf before applying his discount instead of after, which meant that every steady customer paying down a chalk on quarter-day terms had been quietly overcharged the moment they bought anything after their discount fell due. It was not much on any single page. Across a whole street, over a whole quarter, it was considerably more than four marks. She thanked Dill, corrected his account on the spot, and kept the page.

---

**routeA_mid**
She talked it through with Dill twice and still came out somewhere she wasn't confident in, which was not a feeling Marta enjoyed having about her own arithmetic. She corrected his account to the figure that felt soundest, apologised for the trouble, and made a note to come back to the order-of-operations question when she had a clearer head and fewer customers watching her think.

**routeB_setup**
The carter, a broad silent woman named Ossa who never wrote a word she could avoid writing, filled in the delivery register each outfall-day from memory and a stub of chalk, then handed it over for Marta to make sense of. Six carts had come through this quarter and any one of them might have been miscounted at the weighbridge, over-counted at the door, or simply guessed at when the fog made the numbers on the sacks illegible. Marta could not check all six by quarter-day. She could check two, chosen fairly, and she needed the die to do it without anyone accusing her of picking on Ossa's least favourite customer.

---

**routeB_good**
The dice obliged: Cart Three came up flagged, same as the numbers had wanted all along. Against the weighbridge slip, Ossa's tally for that delivery ran four sacks short of what the ledger claimed had come in, sacks that had apparently walked off somewhere between the outfall grate and the storeroom door. Ossa swore several times, none of them at Marta, and re-signed the register there and then with considerably more care than the first time. It was not the whole four marks, but it was a good part of it, and it was in writing.

---

**routeB_mid**
Cart Three never came up. She checked the two carts the dice did give her anyway, out of thoroughness, and found both registers sound, which told her only that the fault, wherever it was, hadn't been generous enough to be caught by chance today. Ossa signed the register again regardless and went back out into the fog looking faintly insulted on the horses' behalf.

**routeC_setup**
Perren had worked the counter on collection days for eight months and had, until recently, seemed entirely uninterested in the arithmetic of it, which was itself unusual for a boy sharp enough to make change without counting on his fingers. Marta had watched him linger near the till twice this month when he had no reason to. She sent the shop empty on a pretext, latched the door, and asked him straight out what he thought he was doing.

A boy telling the truth generally slows down when you press him. A boy telling a lie generally speeds up.

**routeC_good**
He cracked on the third date, not the first, which was almost admirable. He hadn't been skimming, not exactly: he'd been quietly rounding his own wages up by a half-mark here and there whenever he totted the till at close, on the theory that nobody checked a boy's arithmetic as carefully as they checked a customer's. He was right about that, which was rather the problem. Marta didn't shout. She made him write out, in his own hand, every entry he'd touched for the past month, told him the half-marks were coming out of his pay until the book balanced, and said she'd be checking his sums herself every night, for as long as it took her to trust him again.

---

**routeC_mid**
He held his denial all the way to the door and out into the street, and Marta let him go rather than drag a scene out onto Ferrow Lane where every window would be watching by morning. She wasn't certain, only suspicious, and suspicion wasn't proof, whatever the Rise thought about people like her and people like him. She'd be watching the till more closely regardless. That would have to do for now.

---

**route_converge**
Between Dill's discount, Ossa's missing sacks and whatever Perren had or hadn't been doing to his own wages, Marta had enough now to write a single clean page: every figure re-derived from first principles, every discount applied in the right order, every sack accounted for or honestly marked as missing. She called it, privately, the corrected page, and it was worth more to her than the till itself, because a book that could show its own working was a book nobody could easily call unsound. She still needed to account for the weights on the scale and the running total the clerk had quoted her, but this was a start, and a start with paper behind it.

**comp2_setup**
The scale itself came next. If Old Custom and Sons meant to argue her weights were wrong as well as her sums, she wanted to know before they did. A joint delivery of coke had come in that quarter for two households sharing a cart to save on the carter's fee, and the split between them had been argued over at the counter for a full ten minutes before Marta had simply written down a number to make them both go home. She was no longer confident it was the right one.

---

**comp2_good**
Eighteen marks to Widow Tarn, thirty-six to the Pallisers, and not the even split of twenty-seven and twenty-seven Marta had scrawled down in a hurry that day to stop the arguing. She corrected both accounts, wrote the ratio out properly this time in figures nobody could dispute, and set the corrected split alongside the corrected page. It would not by itself satisfy a clerk from the Rise, but a book that mended its own mistakes when shown them was, in her considerable experience, the only kind worth trusting.

**comp2_mid**
She talked herself round to an answer but not to a confident one, and rather than write down a figure she wasn't sure of twice in the same account, she left the even split standing for now, marked the page with a query in the margin, and told herself she'd settle it properly once quarter-day was behind her. It wasn't the cleanest choice she'd made all week. It also wasn't the worst.

**comp3_setup**
One thing remained: the actual figure Fessup had quoted at her, the running arrears he claimed her book showed building over six weeks, the number his whole 'unsound accounts' argument hung on. If she couldn't account for it exactly, ratio and rounding errors and light-fingered shop-boys would count for nothing at quarter-day. She lit a second candle, which she resented on principle, and pulled Tessa Brindle's fair copy of the ledger alongside her own to check them line for line.

**comp3_skillcheck**
Six weeks of entries, added twice, by two different hands on two different nights, and somewhere in that column Fessup had found a number worth building a buyout on. Marta ran her finger down the marks column the way she had a thousand times before, adding as she went, refusing to let herself hurry even with the candle burning down and the Engine's next venting already due.

---

**comp3_success**
There it was, three lines from the bottom: a nine and a one swapped in the carrying, nineteen written where ninety-one belonged, an old man's cramped hand and a young clerk's eagerness to finish a column making exactly the kind of error a debt-factor with a folder full of ribbon would be thrilled to find and never think to question. She corrected it, initialled it, and set the true figure beside Tessa's fair copy, which agreed with her to the mark. Two independent hands, the same number. That was worth more than any single sum, however carefully done.

**comp3_fail**

She copied out her own total, noted honestly where it departed from his, and decided that showing her working plainly would have to serve where certainty wouldn't. It was, she reflected, roughly the situation most of Ferrow Lane found itself in most of the time. She was no stranger to making do.

**forge — forged**

Together they did not just answer Fessup's question, they answered it better than he had asked it, showing exactly where every mark of the supposed arrears had gone and exactly who owed exactly what. She bound the three pages into the front of the ledger itself, ahead of the accounts proper, so that anyone who opened her book from now on would see the working before they saw a single balance. A book that showed its own mistakes and its own corrections was not, whatever Old Custom and Sons wanted to call it, unsound.

She wrote up what she was sure of, flagged what she wasn't, and decided that an honest gap in a ledger had always been worth more to her than a confident lie. **finale**
Fessup came back on quarter-day exactly as he'd promised, ribbon and all, and found a woman with a ledger open to its own working rather than its balance, which was evidently not what Old Custom and Sons trained its clerks to expect. He read her corrections twice, looking for the seam he was used to finding in books like hers, and did not find one, because there wasn't one left to find. He retied his ribbon for the second time that month with the air of a man doing sums of his own, about how much trouble a street this well-kept was actually worth to his employers, and left without a word about receivership. Outside, the Engine ticked its afternoon knock and vented its fog exactly on schedule, indifferent as ever to anyone's arithmetic, and the queue at Marta's counter reformed itself as if nothing had happened, because on Ferrow Lane nothing much ever did, until it very nearly did, and then somebody like Marta simply added the column again until it came right.
