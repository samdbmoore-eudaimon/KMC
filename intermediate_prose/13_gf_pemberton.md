# gf_pemberton — "The Transposed Row"

**tagline**
A tired hand swapped two digits on two dockets, and swapped two futures along with them. Pemberton has until the ink-run bell to swap them back.

---

**intro**
Friday reconciliation. Pemberton liked Fridays the way a man likes the smaller of two available headaches: the boards came down off the wall, the carbons came up out of the drawers, and for two quiet hours nobody wanted anything from him except correct sums, which he could reliably provide.

It had been a fortnight since a Wrought officer with a level voice had asked him four polite questions and walked out knowing about a room he was not supposed to have mentioned. He had tried, and failed, to get the vault's cipher changed before she could use what she'd learned. Nobody had come for him about it yet. He checked the corridor before he checked anything else, these days, and told himself it was just thoroughness.

Thoroughness was, as it happened, how he found it. Board Nine, Stratum V, second column: SLACK, P. 52 marks. Pemberton frowned at the name the way you frown at a coat that doesn't fit the peg. He'd stamped that girl's paper himself, three weeks back, and remembered thinking, with the small private pleasure of a man who loves a clean sum, that whoever P. Slack was, she wrote like someone who'd never once been allowed to be lazy about a fraction.

52 did not sound like that girl. 52 sounded like a mistake wearing her name.

**arithmetic_check**
The master tally sheet ran to nine hundred rows in a clerk's hand gone ragged somewhere around row six hundred, the point in any triple shift where digits stop meaning anything and start just being shapes you're owed home for. Pemberton laid two dockets side by side under the lamp: SLACK, P., number 4179, and two rows below it, DOSS, C., number 4197.

Same four digits. Different order, near the end. The kind of thing a wide-awake clerk catches in a blink and an exhausted one copies faithfully, twice, onto two different boards, and never thinks of again.

Somebody exact needed to check every digit against the carbon stubs before deciding this was worth the trouble it was about to become.

**slate_found**

Wedged behind the tally sheet, forgotten by whoever had last used the drawer, Pemberton found a nine-column slate, chalk-dust worn into every groove, the sort of thing a truly careful predecessor keeps for exactly this. It fit his hand like it had been waiting for someone who'd actually use it properly.

**finale**
The bell went at six exactly, same as it had every evening for as long as anyone in the building could remember, indifferent to what had or hadn't been settled underneath it. Coyle sealed Board Nine and Board Two into the bound ledger with SLACK, P. and DOSS, C. carrying the marks that actually belonged to them, and locked the Bindery for the night without another word.

Pemberton walked home the long way, for once, past the tannery district where a girl he had never met and would probably never meet was, at that exact moment, entirely unaware that a thin, damp, anxious clerk had spent his whole afternoon being braver than he felt on her behalf, over four digits nobody but him had thought worth the trouble.

He did not think of it as bravery. He thought of it, doing the sum honestly as he always did, as simply the job, done properly, for once, all the way through.
