# gf_growl — "The Weight of the Round"

**tagline**
Vane wants an example made on Cinder Row today. Growl means to choose who it gets made of.

---

**intro**
Vane had given Growl the easy half of the job and the boy the hard half, which was Vane's idea of training.

"Take Pitch round with you," Vane had said, not looking up from the ledger. "Cinder Row. The Sirrath girl, the one whose father read gauges on the eastern bank before his lungs gave out. Three marks owing since spring, and word's got round that Ma Sindry's crew are running chalk cheaper than we are. I want it seen paid, one way or another, and I want it seen by teatime." A pause, the kind Vane was good at. "Let the boy do the seeing to."

Pitch was twelve, or near enough, and had been running errands for the Ashlight for eleven days. He had not yet broken anything that mattered to anybody except a shopkeeper.

Growl had watched that particular education happen to other children more times than he cared to count, and had once, a long time ago, been the child it happened to.

"What's she owe it on?"

"Medicine. Her mother's chest." Growl did not slow down. "And there's one thing in that flat worth breaking in front of a street full of witnesses, and it's a clockwork sparrow her father built her before the fog took him. Vane wants it seen. He didn't say what."

Pitch went a little pale, which was the correct response, and said nothing the whole way down Tallow Street.

Growl let him think.

**valve_check**
The stairwell up to Tansy's landing ran past a busted junction pipe nobody in the building had a writ to mend and nobody outside it would mend for free.

It hissed white and constant, and the wall beside it was warm enough to raise a blister on anybody who leaned there without looking.

Ma Sindry's crew would be coming the same way and coming fast, and there was exactly one dry step past that pipe if you read the hiss right, and exactly one scalded shin if you did not.

Somebody with thirteen years of standing next to bad pipework had better go first.

---

**valve_find**
Growl listened to it thicken, thin, thicken again, and went through the gap between the second thickening and the third the way you step between two people arguing who have not noticed you.

Dry. Unscalded. Three seconds ahead of where that pipe would have had him.

Wedged behind the loose brick where it met the wall, left by some long-gone tenant with the same idea, was a heavy leather glove, scorched black across the palm and sound underneath. The sort a gauge-hand wears to read a dial too hot to touch.

"Put your feet where I put mine," Growl told Pitch.

The boy did, and got up without a singed cuff, and looked at his own boots afterwards as though they had done something clever on their own.

The Scald-Reader's Glove is yours. (+1 Science, kept forever.)

---

**valve_miss**
He misjudged the third thickening by half a breath and took the edge of it across one forearm, which raised a welt he would be feeling for a week and did nothing whatever for his temper.

Whatever was behind that brick stayed behind it.

Pitch came up on his own reading of the hiss, which was worse than Growl's and not fatal, and arrived at the top looking faintly proud of himself.

Growl decided not to spoil that.

**gate_fight** (scene intro)
Ma Sindry's crew had beaten them to the landing after all. Two of them, filling the doorway with the particular confidence of people who get paid the same whether the debt is theirs to collect or not.

"Ashlight's slow today," said the bigger one, not moving. "Might be we collect first. Might be we collect different."

Growl had spent thirteen years being the reason other people's crews thought twice about doorways.

He put Pitch behind him without appearing to, which is a skill in itself, and did not raise his voice.

"Move. Or don't, and I'll move you."

**Ma Sindry's Collectors — hitText**
Growl puts one of them into the wall without any particular hurry, and the sound the man makes has real respect buried somewhere in it.

**Ma Sindry's Collectors — strikeText**
A fist comes in low and fast and Growl takes it on the forearm rather than let it past him toward the stairs where Pitch is standing.

**Ma Sindry's Collectors — defeatText**
The second one looks at the first, looks at the stairs, and decides Ma Sindry's coin is not worth this particular landing. They go the way they came, quickly, without the dignity of a proper retreat.

---

**gate_win**
The landing was theirs. Pitch stood very still against the wall, breathing as though he had been the one fighting, and looked at Growl with something that was not quite fear and not quite anything else Growl had a name for.

"You didn't have to hit him that hard."

"No," Growl agreed. "I didn't."

He did not explain the difference between hard enough and too hard. There would be time or there would not, and either way explaining it now would only make it sound cleverer than it was.

**gate_retreat**
The collectors got the better of that particular exchange and Growl pulled Pitch back down half a flight before either of them lost more than pride.

"We're not going up that way." He pressed a hand flat against his ribs. "We're going round. Might take the hour we haven't got."

He did not sound worried about it, which worried Pitch a great deal more than worrying would have.

**crossroads**
An hour, then, and Growl meant to spend every minute of it.

Vane wanted the debt seen paid. Fine. Growl intended to make sure that what got seen and what actually happened were two entirely separate arrangements, and for that he needed something heavy enough to pass for three marks of iron in a hurried weighing, and nobody's business how he came by it.

Three places inside the hour might give him one. The scrapyard on Founder's Cut, where the furnace-men ran a wager Growl had lost money on as a much younger and much stupider Sirrath. The boiler stacks, where the fourth venting was due and the shortcut behind them was only ever safe on a good day. Or Tanner's Row, where a debt of his own, years old and never called in, might finally be worth calling.

---

**scrapyard_setup**
The furnace-men of Founder's Cut ran a wager older than any of them, and it cost nothing to enter and almost nobody ever won it.

Two scrap plates went into the mouth of the furnace side by side, near enough identical to look at. You put your stake on which of the two would go first: which would buckle, or bow, or split along a seam nobody could see from outside.

Growl had lost three marks on that furnace at seventeen and had spent a long time afterwards being annoyed about it in the wrong way, which is to say he had decided it was luck.

He was thirty-one now and he had spent thirteen years standing close to metal that was being asked to do more than it wanted to.

---

**scrapyard_good**
He looked at the two plates for a while before he put anything down.

One had been pickled and the other had not, and you could see it in the way the rust had come off them, and a plate that has had the scale taken off it takes heat evenly and a plate that has not takes it in patches. He had watched that happen to boiler plate on the eastern bank every winter of his working life.

He named the one that would go, and it went, and it went in the place he had said it would go.

The foreman's face did something complicated.

"Nobody's ever called the seam as well as the plate," he said, and it was very nearly an accusation. "You've stood too near a lot of hot things."

"Something like that," said Growl, and took the heap: scrap iron with a rusted axle bearing among it, weighty and dull and exactly the sort of thing that reads as three marks of settled debt to anybody not looking closely.

Component found: Scavenged Axle Bearing.

---

**scrapyard_mid**
He got the plate right and the seam wrong, which the furnace-men counted as a loss and which cost him the stake.

It needled him more than losing three marks at seventeen had, because at seventeen he had not known what he was looking at and now he did, and had still gone with the one that felt right instead of the one he could actually see.

"Come back when you've slept," said the foreman, sweeping the heap into a pile, not unkindly. "Furnace isn't going anywhere. Nor's the plate."

**stacks_setup**
The shortcut behind the boiler stacks ran directly under the Engine's fourth venting of the day: a corridor of scalding white on a schedule nobody controlled and everybody in the Under carried in their knees.

Cross between the third rumble and the fourth and you were through clean. Cross wrong and you spent the walk home explaining a burn nobody would believe was an accident.

Growl had crossed it a hundred times. He still stood at the mouth of it a moment longer than he needed to, every single time, which he had never once admitted to anybody.

---

**stacks_good**
He went through the gap between the third rumble and the fourth without breaking stride, and the fog curled shut behind him like a door closing on somebody else's business.

Half-buried in the ash at the far end, thrown clear of some engine housing years back and never claimed, sat a scavenged axle bearing. Rusted solid on the outside and sound underneath, and heavy enough to fool a hurried tally.

Component found: Scavenged Axle Bearing.

---

**stacks_miss**
The fourth came half a breath early, or he went half a breath late, and the difference was a scald along one arm that would blister by morning and a considerable amount of very quiet swearing in a language nobody in that lane spoke any more.

Whatever was lying in that ash stayed in it.

He came out the far side with nothing but the burn, and the hour running down harder than it had been.

---

**tannery_setup**
Tanner's Row smelled of hide and lye and old debts.

Somewhere in a back room a Laachmi called Coop had owed Growl a favour for eleven years, for a beating he had never had to take, and Growl had never called it in, on the theory that an uncollected debt is worth a good deal more than a collected one.

The theory ran out the moment he walked in and found the door held not by Coop but by the man the guild hired when it wanted its schedules kept.

---

**tannery_fight** (scene intro)
They called him the Clockwork Bailiff on Tanner's Row and it was not a compliment and it was not entirely a joke either.

A heavy, unhurried, entirely reasonable man in a guild coat, who had never in eleven years made an exception, taken a bribe, lost his temper or gone home early, and who greeted Growl with the flat courtesy of somebody reading off a list.

"Registered visits only." He did not move out of the doorway. "State your writ."

"Haven't got one," said Growl, and put his weight on the back foot. "Going to have to make do with the alternative."

"They generally do," said the Bailiff, and rolled his shoulders once, without any apparent feeling about it at all.

**The Clockwork Bailiff — hitText**
Growl finds the one place the man has never had to defend, which is the inside of his own patience, and gets past his guard before the guard has finished deciding.

**The Clockwork Bailiff — strikeText**
A forearm comes round in a slow flat arc and Growl takes it on the shoulder rather than let it get anywhere near the boy in the doorway.

**The Clockwork Bailiff — defeatText**
The Bailiff sits down on a hide bale, without drama, checks his own knuckles, and marks something in a small book, having evidently decided that the schedule can survive one exception after eleven years.

---

**tannery_good**
Coop came out of the back at the sound of the Bailiff sitting down, took one look at Growl, and had the grace not to ask why it had taken eleven years for that favour to come calling.

"Whatever you need."

He meant it, and what Growl needed turned out to be in a crate of guild scrap by the door: a scavenged axle bearing, heavier than it looked, exactly the kind of dead weight that reads as settled debt to anybody not counting closely.

"That's it?" said Coop. "Eleven years for a lump of iron?"

"It's not for the iron," said Growl, and did not explain that either.

Component found: Scavenged Axle Bearing.

---

**tannery_bad**
The Bailiff got the better of the exchange and Growl pulled back rather than let a third one land with Pitch standing in the doorway watching.

Coop shouted something through the back door about coming with proper paperwork next time, which was Coop's way of saying he was sorry.

The crate of guild scrap stayed where it was, on the far side of a man who did not make exceptions.

**converge**
Whichever way the hour had gone, Growl came out of it with something heavy enough to fool a scale, a boy who had stopped asking why, and considerably less time than he had started with.

Two more things and he would have what he actually wanted, which was to give Pitch something better to remember about today than a broken sparrow.

Peg's yard would do the rest, if Peg's yard was still open, and Peg's yard shut the moment the shutters came down for the four o'clock venting.

---

**counter_good**
He came round the last corner with breath enough to knock rather than hammer, which after the day so far felt like luxury.

Peg was already halfway through pulling the shutter and stopped it with one boot, unimpressed and unsurprised in the manner of somebody who had been doing this since before Growl was born.

"Five minutes." A glance at the clock. "Generous of you."

A strip of cured tan-hide came off a hook by the door, tough as old leather gets, meant for binding a grip that was expected to take a beating.

"That's for whatever you're building," said Peg. "Not for whatever you're planning to hit with it. I know the difference in your face, Sirrath, even if you don't."

Component found: Strip of Cured Tan-Hide.

---

**counter_mid**
He made the door with the shutter already halfway down and nothing left in Peg's face that was going to raise it again.

Rules were rules on venting days. Even Peg's own rules. Especially Peg's own rules.

"Tomorrow," Peg called through the gap, not unkindly, and the shutter finished its descent with a clang that meant exactly what it sounded like.

**spring_find**
Growl's hand closed round the coil a hair's breadth from the grate, quicker than the thing had any right to expect from something his size.

It was still warm, and it hummed faintly under his fingers, the way anything does when it has been wound tighter than it was ever meant to sit.

From the yard gate Pitch said, "I didn't even see you move."

It was the first thing the boy had said all day that sounded like a compliment rather than a warning to himself, and Growl was not at all sure which of the two he would rather it had been.

Component found: Wound Valve Coil.

**spring_miss**
The coil went down the grate half a second ahead of his fingers and unspooled into the dark with a diminishing rattle that sounded, if anything, faintly pleased with itself.

"Leave it," Growl said, straightening up, before either of them could suggest going in after it.

Some things you get one clean reach at, and the drains of Cinder Row keep whatever they are given.

**forge** (scene intro)
Peg's forge glowed low and steady behind the yard, the one part of that day that had kept to its own schedule.

"Knuckle-guard, is it." Peg turned the pieces over one at a time on the bench: the bearing, the hide, the coil, or the gaps where they should have been. "For a Sirrath who already hits like a dropped anvil. Bold."

"Not for hitting harder," said Growl. "For hitting exactly as hard as I mean to. There's a difference, and I'd like to stop finding it out the day after."

Peg looked at him for slightly longer than the sentence needed.

**forge — forged**
Peg set the bearing into a housing, wound the coil through the grip to give it something to argue with, and bound the whole thing in tan-hide until it sat across Growl's knuckles like a thing built rather than a thing worn.

"Won't stop a blow landing," said Peg. "Will stop it landing all the way through. That's the whole of the trick, most days, and most men never come in here asking for it."

**finale**
Tansy's door at last, with the street already gathering the way streets do when they can smell an example coming.

Growl put the axle bearing down on her table with a clang loud enough for the whole landing to hear, and made a considerable performance of weighing three marks of scrap against three marks of chalk on Vane's borrowed scale, saying a good deal about interest and lateness for the benefit of anybody listening at the door.

Underneath the noise, quieter than the scale, he counted the actual remainder out of his own coat and told Tansy to chalk it paid and say nothing about who had settled it.

The clockwork sparrow sat on the shelf behind her the entire time, wings folded, untouched. Nobody outside that room would ever know it had been in any danger at all, which was the point of the whole afternoon.

"That's it?" Pitch asked, once they were back on the stairs. "That's the lesson?"

"The lesson is that you can make a street believe you've broken something without breaking it." Growl went down two steps and stopped. "Remember that one longer than anything else I teach you, because everything else I teach you is going to be worse."

He did not say the rest of it.

The rest of it was that eleven years ago somebody had done something not unlike this for a Sirrath boiler-hand two streets over, and steered him toward a workshop instead of a recruitment, and Growl still did not know whether that had been mercy or a man placing a bet against himself years too late.

Some lessons you pass on before you have finished learning them.

**end — goodText**
Word got back to Vane, as word always did, that the Cinder Row debt had been seen paid and seen paid hard, and Vane was satisfied in the specific way of a man who only ever checks that the noise was loud enough and never what it was made of.

Tansy's sparrow kept its wings.

Pitch walked home a different shape of quiet than he had walked out in, turning over something he was not ready to say out loud yet and might not be for years.

Growl clocked back in at the Ashlight that night the same enforcer he had been that morning, on paper, in the eyes of everybody who signed his wages. Not redeemed. Not finished with the job, and with no plans to be.

Just angry on somebody else's behalf again, and reasonably sure that this time the anger had gone somewhere it was some use.

**end — midText**
The debt got paid down further than Vane had any right to expect and not so far as Growl would have liked.

The sparrow came through with one dented wing, straightened with a pair of pliers before anybody in the street got close enough to see, and Tansy's mother never learned how near it had come to worse.

Pitch went home rattled and mostly silent, which was its own kind of lesson learned and a rougher one than Growl had meant to teach on a first round.

He clocked back in with Vane that night the same as ever. Still the Ashlight's fist. Still working for people he had no illusions about whatever.

Still, in his own slow and unfinished way, trying to work out where hard enough stops.
