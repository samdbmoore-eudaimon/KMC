# Junior module: KS3 and UKMT curriculum audit

Date: 31 August 2026

## Implementation status: 1 September 2026

The recommended overhaul has now been implemented for the whole regular Junior module. The visible catalogue contains 26 KS3 curriculum topics and seven clearly marked UKMT logic extensions. Every visible topic has a lesson, a D1-D4 generator and five registered question structures. Logic extensions remain available in lessons and practice but enter mocks only from Level 7. Existing Olympiad training and its separate question bank were not changed.

The focused progression audit now reports 33 visible topics, 173 teaching sections, 692 worked examples and 165 globally unique registered structures, with no findings. The earlier counts and gaps below are retained as the evidence-led before-state that motivated the rewrite.

## Executive finding

The concern is justified. The existing Junior module is rich in mathematical puzzles, but its organising principle is mostly a catalogue of recurring competition mechanisms rather than a coherent Key Stage 3 curriculum.

The current module contains 45 lesson-generator pairs. Many are useful question families, but they are presented as curriculum topics: examples include `sportScore`, `calendar`, `truthLiars`, `seating`, `magicGrid`, `poolPath`, `epicJourney` and `digitDetective`. At the same time, several statutory KS3 areas have no clear teaching home: linear graphs, constructions, probability, statistical analysis, formal algebraic manipulation, trigonometry, standard form and broader graphical interpretation.

The recommended response is a substantial reorganisation, not a deletion of the good material. Junior should be rebuilt around the 26 connected curriculum blocks in the Department for Education's KS3 teaching guidance. Existing contest mechanisms should be retained as verbal structures and reasoning routes inside those topics. A smaller number should remain as clearly labelled enrichment strands.

In short:

- The curriculum should determine the topics.
- UKMT evidence should determine the style, depth and reasoning routes within each topic.
- A historical UKMT question archetype should not normally become a topic merely because it has appeared more than once.
- Regular Junior Maths should prepare a player for KS3 mathematics first, with JMC and Junior Kangaroo shaping how that mathematics is applied in unfamiliar ways.
- JMO is a separate stretch pathway. Its papers shape the existing Olympiad training section and its separate question set, not the regular topics, practice generators or mocks.

## Research scope and method

### Curriculum sources

The curriculum spine was taken from the statutory [National curriculum in England: mathematics programme of study for key stage 3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7c1408e5274a1f5cc75a68/SECONDARY_national_curriculum_-_Mathematics.pdf) and checked against the Department for Education's 295-page [Mathematics guidance: Key Stage 3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1033574/Mathematics_guidance_key_stage_3.pdf).

The statutory programme requires fluency, mathematical reasoning and problem solving. It also says pupils should move between numerical, algebraic, graphical and diagrammatic representations, make and test conjectures, begin deductive proof and apply familiar mathematics to unfamiliar and non-routine problems.

The 2021 guidance is especially relevant to this audit. It recommends a coherent sequence of connected ideas, warns against superficial coverage of many disconnected topics and explicitly treats algebra as a generalisation of number and multiplicative reasoning as the connection between fractions, percentages, ratio, scale and proportion.

### Competition corpus

The paper window is 2017 to 2026 inclusive: the latest ten completed annual rounds at the time of this audit.

- 10 Junior Mathematical Challenge papers: 250 multiple-choice problems.
- 9 Junior Kangaroo papers: 225 multiple-choice problems. There was no 2020 follow-on round.
- 9 Junior Mathematical Olympiad papers. Before 2025 each paper contained ten short Section A problems and six full-solution Section B problems. From 2025 the short section was removed and the paper became six full written solutions. This gives 124 JMO problems in the window.
- Total reviewed corpus: 28 papers and 599 problems, plus available official solutions, investigations and marker reports.

The official archive and competition descriptions are available through the [UKMT papers and solutions archive](https://ukmt.org.uk/competition-papers/), [Junior Mathematical Challenge](https://ukmt.org.uk/junior-challenges/junior-mathematical-challenge), [Junior Kangaroo](https://ukmt.org.uk/junior-challenges/junior-kangaroo) and [Junior Mathematical Olympiad](https://ukmt.org.uk/junior-challenges/junior-mathematical-olympiad) pages.

This is a curriculum-design coding of the corpus rather than a claim that every problem has one exclusive topic. Good UKMT problems deliberately combine ideas, so the audit records primary content, secondary content, representation and reasoning route. That is also the right model for the generators.

## What KS3 requires

The statutory subject content is grouped into six domains:

1. Number
2. Algebra
3. Ratio, proportion and rates of change
4. Geometry and measures
5. Probability
6. Statistics

Across those domains, the current Junior design must eventually teach at least the following:

- positive and negative integers, decimals, fractions, place value and ordering
- factors, multiples, primes, HCF, LCM, prime factorisation, powers, roots and operation priority
- exact fraction work, percentages, approximation, rounding, error intervals and standard form
- algebraic notation, substitution, equivalence, collecting, expansion, factorisation, formulae and linear equations
- coordinates, sequences, linear and simple quadratic graphs, gradient and intercept and graphical solutions
- ratios, scaling, maps, direct and inverse proportion, percentage change, simple interest and compound units
- perimeter, area, circles, volume, constructions, angle facts, polygons, transformations, congruence and similarity
- Pythagoras and right-angled trigonometry
- probability scales, sample spaces, tables, grids, Venn diagrams and combined events
- statistical tables and charts, averages and spread, grouped data, comparison of distributions and scatter graphs

The equally important process requirement is that players use these ideas flexibly: choosing operations, changing representation, explaining why, testing cases, proving impossibility and modelling multi-step situations.

## What the UKMT papers actually reward

### Junior Mathematical Challenge

The JMC uses accessible mathematics but disguises the required method. Across the decade, the most persistent content is:

- integer arithmetic, divisibility, factors, digit properties and remainders
- fractions, ratios, percentages, rates and unit conversion
- angles, polygons, perimeter, area and spatial visualisation
- sequences, repeated processes and pattern recognition
- translating short stories into equations or systematic cases
- counting arrangements and eliminating impossible cases

Question position is an approximate difficulty signal. Early questions usually test one clean observation or calculation. Middle questions require a representation change or two linked facts. Late questions often require casework, an invariant, an extremal argument, reverse reasoning or a non-obvious geometric decomposition.

### Junior Kangaroo

Junior Kangaroo uses broadly the same mathematical content but expects greater depth and compression. Compared with the JMC, it places more weight on:

- multi-step number and ratio reasoning
- systematic counting and arrangements
- diagram interpretation and spatial reasoning
- interacting constraints rather than one named procedure
- identifying an efficient route instead of attempting exhaustive calculation

This supports harder structures within curriculum topics. It does not support creating a separate curriculum topic for every unusual story or puzzle form.

### Junior Mathematical Olympiad

The JMO changes the required output. It repeatedly draws on elementary number theory, algebraic representation, geometry, combinatorics, extremal reasoning, parity, invariants and proof by exhaustion or contradiction. The distinctive skill is not a separate body of school content: it is constructing a complete argument.

From 2025, all six questions require full written solutions. UKMT guidance is explicit that a correct final answer with little or no reasoning earns very little credit. This evidence belongs to the separate Olympiad training section and question bank. It should inform that section's strategy teaching, proof-writing models and marking guidance, but it should not define regular-topic D4 or enter ordinary practice and mock generation.

Regular lessons should still explain their worked solutions fully. That is good teaching and supports the statutory KS3 reasoning aims, but a fully explained curriculum solution is not the same thing as importing an Olympiad problem into the regular generator.

### The important curriculum conclusion

The papers under-sample some statutory KS3 content. Formal graph work, statistical representation, constructions, standard form and trigonometry are naturally rarer in a no-calculator competition for Year 8 and below. Their rarity in historical papers is not a reason to omit them from a KS3 module.

UKMT should therefore be used as a reasoning and assessment layer over the curriculum, not as the curriculum itself.

## Current Junior inventory

The current local module has:

- 40 ordinary topics and 5 deep topics
- 45 generators and 45 matching lessons
- 469 lesson sections
- 698 complete worked examples
- 239 sections with no worked examples
- 222 sections with three worked examples
- 8 sections with four worked examples, all in `fractionUnusual`
- 0 lesson examples linked to a generator `structureId`
- 0 generator outputs exposing a `structureId` or `variantId` in the sampled audit

The material is not empty or poor. It contains a large amount of useful explanation and many valid generators. The problem is that it cannot currently demonstrate the required relationship between curriculum progression, D1-D4, verbal structures and lesson examples.

Only `fractionUnusual` currently meets the new four-examples-per-section lesson count. Even there, the worked examples are not linked to named generator structures, so the lesson-generator relationship is not auditable.

Exact-question freshness exists, but it is based on topic plus complete question text. It prevents many literal repeats but does not prevent two questions with the same reasoning and verbal structure from appearing in one mock. The fallback can also accept a repeat if generation is exhausted. Mock non-repetition therefore needs to operate on structure and variant metadata, not only exact wording.

## Recommended topic architecture

Use the 26 blocks above as the default Junior topic list and sequence. They already form a defensible KS3 progression, connect to KS2 prerequisites and lead into KS4.

The module does not need 45 visible practice topics. It needs approximately 26 curriculum topics, each broad enough to support genuine D1-D4 growth and at least five reasoning structures at each difficulty where mathematically appropriate.

### Content that should become enrichment rather than curriculum topics

These are valuable, but should be labelled as cross-topic problem-solving or considered for the separate Olympiad pathway:

- `truthLiars`
- `pigeonhole` as a theorem and proof method beyond its probability applications
- `magicGrid`
- `gridLogic`
- `networkGraph`
- the generic “deep” labels once their useful structures have been distributed across curriculum topics

They can remain in the separate Olympiad academy and question bank, or in another clearly labelled enrichment area. They must not enter the ordinary D4 pool merely because they are challenging and must not displace statutory probability, statistics, graphs or algebra from the visible curriculum.

## The six regular-topic reasoning lenses

Every regular generator structure should record one primary reasoning route. These lenses are supported by KS3, JMC and Junior Kangaroo evidence and should recur across many curriculum topics:

1. Translate and model: turn words, a diagram or a table into mathematics.
2. Work backwards and use inverse operations.
3. Enumerate systematically: cases, tables, organised lists or sample spaces.
4. Find structure: parity, divisibility, remainders, symmetry, invariants and repeated behaviour.
5. Bound or optimise: extremes, pigeonhole arguments, largest/smallest and impossibility.
6. Deduce and justify: linked reasons, counterexamples and complete explanations at an appropriate curriculum level.

This is where the present contest expertise should be preserved. For example, Sport Scores is not a curriculum topic, but a sport-table structure can teach linear equations, statistics, systematic enumeration or proportional reasoning.

## Lesson audit against the agreed standard

The target standard is:

- a well-written lesson for every visible topic
- a clear D1-D4 learning progression
- exactly four fully worked examples in every teaching section
- full question, explicit steps and answer for each example
- appropriately detailed reasoning, with every inference explained
- representative examples from each relevant difficulty
- every example linked to a valid generator `structureId`
- direct links to prerequisite lessons, including Primary lessons where appropriate
- some of the same verbal structures used in teaching and generation

Current result:

- 45 of 45 generators have a lesson key.
- All 698 existing worked examples have a question, at least one step and an answer.
- 1 of 45 lessons has four examples in every section.
- 0 of 698 examples has a `structureId`.
- Most lessons alternate explanatory sections with zero examples and worked sections with three. Under the new standard, either every retained teaching section needs four examples or the schema must explicitly distinguish non-teaching introduction/summary blocks from audited teaching sections.
- Difficulty is usually implied by example order rather than declared and linked to D1-D4.

Recommendation: preserve strong prose where useful, but rebuild lesson structure around the new curriculum topic, its prerequisite chain, its four difficulty descriptions and its registered question structures. Do not spend tokens expanding a lesson that will later be merged into another topic.

## Generator audit against the agreed standard

The target standard is:

- four real difficulty bands, D1-D4, with every structure becoming genuinely harder
- at least five distinct verbal structures per difficulty where the mathematics allows it
- a structure may extend across difficulties, but higher use must add mathematical or reasoning demand
- approximately one half story contexts, one third diagrams and one sixth direct questions across a suitable topic; diagram-dependent topics may deviate
- varied international settings with the 9fo world flavour and 9fo character names
- every output carries `structureId`, `variantId`, `representation`, `difficulty` and stretch information where relevant
- each structure records curriculum objective, prerequisite, reasoning route and valid difficulties
- complete worked solutions for every generated question
- exact, mathematical and structural non-repetition inside a mock

Current result:

- All 45 generators accept D1-D4 and generate marked multiple-choice questions with solutions.
- Difficulty branches exist, but there is no registry of structures and no machine-auditable description of what changes from D1 to D4.
- Sampled outputs expose no structure or variant identifiers.
- Several generators clearly have fewer than five stable reasoning structures at one or more difficulty bands, while others create apparent variety by changing names and numbers inside one formulation.
- Representation mix cannot be audited because outputs do not consistently declare `story`, `diagram` or `direct`.
- Exact text freshness is implemented, but structure-level mock freshness is not.

Recommendation: do not rewrite all generators from zero. First introduce one shared structure registry contract, then migrate each new curriculum topic by wrapping, adapting or combining useful existing builders. Delete or retire old keys only after progress migration and content references have been mapped.

## Difficulty model for Junior

Difficulty must represent mathematical progression, not merely larger numbers.

- D1: secure the prerequisite and recognise one representation; one principal step.
- D2: apply the KS3 method in a familiar or lightly disguised setting; two linked steps or one representation change.
- D3: choose the method, connect two ideas or handle a constraint; multi-step JMC middle-to-late-paper reasoning.
- D4: non-routine KS3 synthesis, interacting constraints, efficient casework, invariants, extremal reasoning or Junior Kangaroo-style extension. It remains within the regular curriculum pathway and does not draw questions from the JMO bank.

JMO difficulty is separate from D1-D4. The Olympiad pathway has its own progression, strategy lessons, full-solution questions and marking expectations.

Each topic should state exactly how each structure grows. A story being longer, a number being larger or a distractor being closer does not by itself create a new difficulty.

## Recommended migration order

Work in batches of five, preserving useful builders and lesson prose.

1. Foundation batch: Place value; Properties of number; Arithmetic with integers and decimals; Fractions; Expressions and equations.
2. Representation batch: Coordinates; Perimeter and area; Multiplicative relationships; Transformations; Estimation and rounding.
3. Relationship batch: Sequences; Linear graphs; Linear equations; Percentages and proportionality; Statistical representations and measures.
4. Reasoning batch: Statistical analysis; Perimeter, area and volume; Polygons; Constructions; Similarity and Pythagoras.
5. Higher KS3 batch: Probability; Non-linear relationships; Expressions and formulae; Trigonometry; Standard form.
6. Final graph and separation batch: Graphical representations, then redistribute or retire the remaining contest-labelled keys. Audit the Olympiad pathway separately without mixing its questions into the new regular generators.

For each batch:

1. Write the topic specification and prerequisite links.
2. Inventory reusable lesson paragraphs and generator builders.
3. Define D1-D4 and at least five structures per difficulty.
4. Build the generator registry first, preserving useful code.
5. Write or reshape the lesson around the registered structures.
6. Add four linked examples to each teaching section.
7. Record old-to-new progress migration.
8. Run focused syntax and sampling checks. Full regression can wait until all topic batches are migrated unless a shared interface changes.

## Final judgement

A major rewrite of the Junior information architecture is warranted. A wholesale rewrite of every useful explanation and question builder is not.

The strongest route is consolidation:

- replace 45 puzzle-labelled topics with about 26 curriculum-led topics
- preserve the best existing puzzles as structures within those topics
- add the missing statutory KS3 content even where UKMT papers rarely test it
- use JMC and Kangaroo to shape unfamiliar application and difficulty
- keep JMO research and content in the separate Olympiad training section and question set
- make every lesson-generator relationship explicit through shared structure IDs

That produces a genuine learning progression and avoids training players to recognise one historical UKMT formulation.
