# Little Reckoning Expeditions: ten-map route

> Central entry point: `EXPEDITIONS_MASTER_BRIEF.md`. This document preserves curriculum and
> implementation sequencing. The individual map set-pieces and asset intent are superseded by
> `EXPEDITIONS_EXPERIENCE_AND_ART_BRIEF.md` and `EXPEDITIONS_SCENARIO_ART_MATRIX.md`.

Status: proposed implementation brief. Little Reckoning is for ages 10–13. It retains the
same short grid tactics as Ninefold Orchard, but expects pupils to work with negative numbers,
fractions, ratios, bounds, operation order and linked constraints. A normal clear targets five
minutes and may use five rounds, never exceeding eight minutes.

## What changes after Ninefold Orchard

The player still moves a three-card party on a visible hex grid with two actions each. The
difficulty comes from interacting rules, not from longer enemy turns or much larger boards.

| Ninefold Orchard | Little Reckoning |
|---|---|
| Concrete weights and whole-number totals | Signed values, fractions, estimates and short expressions |
| Every intent visible immediately | Core intent visible after the first action; Logic can reveal conditions earlier |
| Usually one objective layer | Two linked conditions, such as value plus capacity or route plus order |
| One new verb per early map | Familiar tactical verb plus one mathematical transformation |
| 4 rounds, 25–39 hexes | 5 rounds, 31–49 hexes |

No map should hide a convention. If a tile changes an operation, reverses a route or uses a
bound, it displays that rule on the object and previews the result before commitment.

## Map 1: The Smudged Stones

**Win:** restore the three missing operators so all marked paths evaluate to 24.

**Mathematics:** choose from `+`, `−`, `×` and `÷` on short visible paths. The teaching
template uses integers and exact division only. Every intermediate result is shown, so a child
can see why an operator fails.

**Pressure:** Fog-thumbs move after the first player action and obscure the nearest unguarded
operator. An obscured operator still works but cannot be changed until revealed or guarded.

**Card lesson:** Logic's Reframe changes an operator and Predict reveals the next Fog-thumb
target. A Guardian can preserve the final needed operator. Stronger cards make interception
and optional combat more reliable, but the puzzle is the point.

**Variation:** choose from pre-solved three-path templates yielding 18–30; rotate the board,
select one initially obscured wheel and choose a Fog-thumb entry route. Require one intended
operator set and at least one readable false trail.

## Map 2: Roughly at the Market

**Win:** collect goods worth at least 50 while carrying no more than 20 weight.

**Mathematics:** estimate intervals for labelled goods, then satisfy both a minimum value and a
maximum weight. For example, `£9.50–£10.50` and weight `4` means a child reasons with a lower
bound when safety matters. Values stay friendly enough for mental arithmetic.

**Pressure:** two Mist Vendors remove one unguarded item each round. Their next stall is
shown once the first action is taken.

**Card lesson:** an Arithmetic visible check can certify a bound before a purchase. Speed
reaches a changing stall. A Scout mark lets the party learn an item route earlier. There are
multiple valid baskets, but weak movement or poor estimates leave no safety margin.

**Variation:** select five of eight goods from pre-verified estimate sets. Solver must prove
at least two valid selections, one tempting overweight selection and one tempting
under-value selection.

## Map 3: The Backwards Courtyard

**Win:** complete the displayed reversed sequence and reach the bell without stepping on a
value that cannot follow the previous one.

**Mathematics:** sequences and inverse operations, initially using `21, 13, 8, 5, 3, 2, 1`.
Later variants use a stated subtract-or-divide recurrence rather than an unexplained pattern.

**Pressure:** the Reverse Knight undoes the most recent unguarded correct step. The map
previews which step is at risk before the end of the round.

**Card lesson:** Logic can Put in Order to repair a route tile. Guard protects the last step.
Speed can reach the bell via an outer route before a reversal. This teaches that sequence
reasoning and positioning are one plan.

**Variation:** use only recurrence templates with one unambiguous predecessor at every step.
Choose one reversal moment and mirror the courtyard. Never require guessing the rule.

## Map 4: Lanterns in the Forgetting Fog

**Win:** connect four lanterns to the signal post with the minimum stated route cost.

**Mathematics:** a small weighted network. Each route marker has a visible orientation and
cost. The child connects every lantern while comparing total route length, not merely drawing
any connected network.

**Pressure:** Foglings move towards the lowest-cost unguarded route marker and close it for
one round. Their target category appears after the first action; Logic reveals the exact one.

**Card lesson:** Geometry and Reckoner actions rotate route markers. Logic protects against
the wrong assumption. A faster card can physically cover a remote marker. The winning network
must still obey the mathematical cost condition.

**Variation:** hand-authored four-lantern topology templates with two plausible networks and
one unique minimum-cost result. Randomise one marker orientation and Fogling entry lane only.

## Map 5: General Guesswork's Lines

**Win:** clear the General's three-figure formation before the fifth round.

**Mathematics:** attacks are valid only when the chosen estimate interval contains a shown
quantity's true value. The player creates or selects an interval, then compares its endpoints
to the displayed condition. This is combat as numerical reasoning, not a damage race.

**Pressure:** guards defend lanes while an archer marks the most exposed hero. Each enemy has
low hearts but different Defence and interval condition.

**Card lesson:** an Arithmetic-primary striker provides reliable power once the interval is
valid. Scouts mark an enemy for support. Logic reveals the archer's conditional target. A
high-stat card alone cannot clear all three lanes within five rounds without correct intervals
and supporting position.

**Variation:** pre-validated value/interval sets using whole numbers and tenths. Each seed
must give a valid attack plan for every enemy and at least one wrong interval that visibly
misses for mathematical, not random, reasons.

## Map 6: A Shape Coming Apart

**Win:** seal six unstable edges while the two marked regions retain equal perimeter.

**Mathematics:** count unit edges around two changing shapes. Sealing an edge changes one or
both perimeters. The board displays both totals continuously.

**Pressure:** the Unshape breaks one predicted unguarded edge each round. Its priority is
fully visible after the first action.

**Card lesson:** Geometry's Shape Field reaches a coherent run of edges. Science Stabilise
holds an edge through the enemy phase. Guardians protect a vital boundary. Stronger Geometry
increases the visible span of Shape Field, creating a real late-collection advantage.

**Variation:** use hand-authored symmetric region templates, then choose one of three broken
edge schedules. Solver proves equal perimeter remains achievable after every allowed break.

## Map 7: The Countless Crossing

**Win:** build one route that visits every required group exactly once and reaches the exit.

**Mathematics:** set membership and route constraints. Tiles belong to groups such as
multiple-of-3, square number, negative or prime. A tile may belong to two groups, but the
route must satisfy each group requirement exactly once.

**Pressure:** the Countless Swarm changes the cost of the last group entered, making a greedy
short route poor on the next turn.

**Card lesson:** Logic reveals the next altered cost, Geometry bypasses one obstacle on an
exact line and Speed preserves the action budget. A strong party is needed because the three
members must occupy or activate separate route stations in the final round.

**Variation:** generate only from audited group-label templates with a single intended route
class. Values range from -12 to 36 and each label has a clear accessible text description.

## Map 8: Madam Nought's House

**Win:** reduce three locked expressions to zero, release the courier and escort them to the
exit.

**Mathematics:** simplify short signed expressions using inverse operations. Each lock has a
visible current value and one of two available transformations. Solving one lock may change a
linked term elsewhere.

**Pressure:** Nought Shadows re-inflate the most recent unguarded zero lock. They do not hide
the rule: their intention names the lock and its next value.

**Card lesson:** Arithmetic transforms values, Logic locks a correct deduction and Science
stabilises a zero. The rescue requires a hero to leave the locks after solving them, forcing a
team rather than a single specialist.

**Variation:** select linked-expression triples from a bank with integer solutions and a
minimum two-step derivation per lock. Verify the chosen set has no accidental one-action win.

## Map 9: The Almost Mirror

**Win:** make both halves of the courtyard congruent at the end of the same round.

**Mathematics:** reflections, rotation and congruence on a hex grid. The player matches shape,
orientation and labelled side values. An Echo copies the preceding player change imperfectly:
it rotates one extra step or changes one selected value by one.

**Pressure:** two Echoes operate on opposite sides, so the player must predict the imperfect
copy rather than repair one half endlessly.

**Card lesson:** Geometry rotates and shifts pieces. Logic predicts which imperfect copy will
occur. Science can freeze one correct half. Three separate stations must be maintained at the
round boundary, demanding a genuinely strong Epic party.

**Variation:** use a small set of audited reflection templates with exactly one intended final
configuration under each Echo rule. Do not generate arbitrary shape puzzles.

## Map 10: Counted In

**Win:** assign every fragment to exactly one valid region of a five-region partition and hold
the completed board for one enemy phase.

**Mathematics:** fractions, ratios and set partitioning. Every region publishes its rule, such
as `multiples of 3`, `between -2 and 4`, or `equivalent to 1/2`. Some fragments satisfy more
than one apparent rule, but only one placement allows every region to be filled consistently.

**Pressure:** Muddle Fragments try to move an unguarded piece into a tempting wrong region.
Their targets are visible. The player can defeat, guard, lock or re-route around them.

**Card lesson:** Logic Deduce locks a proven placement, Arithmetic handles equivalence,
Geometry moves fragments across a constrained line and Science stabilises a filled region.
The final round requires three heroes to secure different regions at once, so an all-Epic
party has a clear practical advantage.

**Variation:** generate from pre-solved five-region templates. The generator may permute
fragment locations and equivalent representations, but must retain one valid full assignment,
an explanation trace and a minimum of two plausible wrong placements.

## Implementation and validation sequence

1. Preserve The Smudged Stones as the operator-wheel teaching map.
2. Implement Roughly at the Market only after the engine supports two independent tracked
   quantities and visible bound checks.
3. Implement Backwards Courtyard only after `orderedRoute` increments and validates route
   progress in the engine.
4. Treat Lanterns in the Forgetting Fog as a connection-map proof; upgrade it to minimum-cost
   completion only when the optimiser can prove cost, not just connectivity.
5. Build Maps 5–10 after the visible-check primitive and their individual solver fixtures are
   green. No procedural topology is accepted without a replayable solution.
