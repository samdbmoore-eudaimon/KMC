# GIFFORD CARD ART — COMPLETE GENERATION RULESET

All 40 cards share the same **STYLE**, **FACTION PALETTE**, **RARITY LIGHTING**, and **NEGATIVE RULES**. Each card adds a specific **LIGHTING** line and **PALETTE** line.

---

## UNIVERSAL RULES (ALL CARDS)

### STYLE BLOCK (verbatim, all cards)
> Still frame from a 3D animated series for adults. Stylised 3D character forms with hand-painted texture maps. Hard-edged geometry, crisp readable silhouette, near-cel shading with clean shadow terminators. Painted texture inside the shadow shapes only. Matte-painted background with atmospheric depth. High saturation, strong complementary colour separation, motivated cinematic lighting, strong coloured rim light, shallow depth of field with bokeh, fine film grain.
>
> Face construction: large eyes with visible painted iris detail and a specular highlight, strong brow and cheekbone planes, small refined noses, clearly defined lip shapes, slightly exaggerated jaw and neck geometry. Stylised, not naturalistic, and not caricature or goblin either.

### STYLE REFERENCE NOTE (for cards using Corvain reference)
> A reference image is attached. Use it ONLY for its rendering style — line quality, cel shading, rim-light treatment, matte-surface material quality, overall stylisation level. Do NOT reuse its colour palette, its character, its pose, its wardrobe or its environment. Generate an entirely new subject and scene, described below, rendered in that same style.

### FACTION PALETTE BLOCKS (state the relevant block for every card)

**The Rise (Wrought):**
Saturated oxblood, warm cream, polished brass, deep bottle green, tarnished silver. Hard warm key light from above, cool cyan-grey fill. High ceilings, empty air. Architecture is Georgian, not Art Nouveau: straight lines, sash windows, plain cornices, restrained brass — no sweeping organic curves, no filigree.

**The Under (Rustbound):**
Hot rust orange, sodium yellow, cold iron blue-grey, soot black, bright steam white. Key light from below or from one bare source, with cold blue bounce. Air always carrying grit, smoke or fog. No green, no neon green, no toxic teal, no chemical glow — this city runs on steam and rust, not chemtech.

**Neutral/Independent:**
Both factions' palettes split hard across the frame or across the face, warm on one side and cold on the other — or, if the card specifies otherwise (setting overrides faction rules), follow the card's specific lighting description.

### RARITY SIGNALLING THROUGH LIGHT (all cards)
- **Common:** flat, undramatic light from a single everyday source. Mood is plain and ordinary, not cinematic, but faces stay clearly visible and brightly lit.
- **Uncommon:** one strong, purposeful light source creating readable shadow separation.
- **Rare:** two visibly contesting light sources (key and fill of clearly different colours and directions).
- **Epic:** full cinematic rim lighting and depth — both key and fill clearly visible, subject visibly separated from background by rim light.
- **Legendary:** scale, atmosphere and a sense of the frame being too small for the subject. Vast or iconic settings; light is atmospheric, not portrait-focused.

### EXPOSURE RULE (overrides all other lighting descriptions)
Whatever mood words appear elsewhere ("dim", "cramped", "dark", "nighttime"), the actual rendered brightness of the subject's FACE must be HIGH — comparable to a well-lit stage portrait. Character faces must sit in clearly visible, well-exposed light-to-midtone brightness, never near-black or lost in shadow, regardless of skin tone. Darker-skinned characters receive a deliberate, sufficient, unambiguous key or rim light aimed at the face so their expression and skin texture are as easy to read as pale-skinned characters in the same scene. If face legibility and mood-darkness ever conflict, legibility always wins.

### NEGATIVE BLOCK (verbatim, all cards)
> No oil painting, no canvas texture, no impasto, no visible brushstrokes on outlines, no Old Master or Salon portrait, no chiaroscuro gallery portrait, no sepia, no monochrome, no desaturated umber wash, no soft blended edges, no flat ambient studio lighting. No low-poly or geometric faceted faces — the face is smooth stylised human anatomy with painted shadow texture, not a faceted abstraction. No generic realtime-game-engine 3D-character look, no glossy PBR material shading, no photoreal skin shader, no plasticky specular skin — shading is flat cel-style planes with painted texture living inside shadow shapes only.
>
> This is a gothic Victorian steampunk world with no electricity. No electric lighting of any kind, no light bulbs, no modern desk lamps, no LED or fluorescent light quality. Every light source must be gaslight, oil lamp, candle flame, or the setting's own brass/steam machinery glow (furnace light, dial-glow, etc).
>
> Also: no clean modern surfaces, no recognisable real people, no real-world branding, no cute or chibi proportions, no gore, no character design that reads as an existing franchise.

### SIRRATH ANATOMY (for Marrow, Growl, Scarrow — mandatory for all three)
80 per cent human, 20 per cent tiger. Start from a HUMAN face and lay feline traits over the surface. Never start from a tiger and humanise it.

**Human, non-negotiable:** flat human facial plane with NO muzzle and NO snout. Human cranium proportion, high forehead. Human nose shape and bridge. Human mouth with lips. Human chin and jawline. Human-set eyes at human spacing and human scale. Human cheekbones. Human ear placement.

**Feline, only these:** short pelt over the whole of it instead of skin. Dark nose leather at the tip of an otherwise human nose. Ears rounded, set slightly higher and further back than a human's, canted slightly outward. Stripes following human facial planes: across the forehead, at the temples, along the cheekbones, banding the neck. Sparse short whiskers. Feline iris colour. Subtle ruff of longer fur at the jawline.

**Explicit negatives:** no muzzle, no snout, no projecting jaw, no feline mouth line, no plush or fluffy fur, no enlarged eyes, no glossy specular highlights, no wolf, no fox, no dog, no cartoon animal, no Pixar proportions. Should read as a person with a tiger's colouring, not an animal in clothes.

### LAACHMI ANATOMY (for Whistle — mandatory)
Tall, heavy, horned, long-faced, and **dignified rather than monstrous**. Human facial architecture, no snout, with heavy brow ridges, a longer human jaw, dark hide instead of skin, forward-curving horns. Specify intelligence in the face or the generator will hand you a beast. Whistle is a clerk in half-spectacles who happens to be seven feet tall.

#### ROOKE
*Protagonist. Rustbound. Age 14. Girl.*

**Faction block:** The Under (Rustbound)

**Subject:**
A fourteen-year-old girl, small and slight but wiry. FEMALE STRUCTURE MANDATORY: soft jaw tapering to a small pointed chin, narrow neck, narrow shoulders, high rounded cheekbones, thinner arched brows (not heavy), large dark eyes with visible lashes, small mouth. **Palette:**
Cold slate blue-grey and bone white dominant, one saturated warm brass accent. The coldest card in the set. Keep midtones light enough that she reads clearly at thumbnail size — do not render too dark overall.

**Rarity/reference:**
Use Corvain as style reference. Epic rarity lighting (full cinematic rim light and depth).

#### CORVAIN

**Rarity/reference:**
This card IS the style anchor for the entire set. Do NOT regenerate her from text. Use her image as a reference image for all other cards' style.

#### MARROW
*Protagonist. Sirrath. Rustbound. Age 17.*

**Faction block:** The Under (Rustbound)

**Subject:**
Seventeen years old, six foot four. Broad through chest and shoulders, visibly not yet used to that size — posture slightly hunched and self-contained.

**HEAD ANATOMY — 80% HUMAN, 20% TIGER (read Sirrath Anatomy section above):**
Broad flat skull. Ears rounded, set wide and low on sides of head. Short broad muzzle, wide across the bridge (NO — wait, the brief was corrected: "NO MUZZLE. Flat human facial plane with NO muzzle and NO snout of any kind"). Ice-blue eyes. Heavy whisker pads with visible whiskers. Wide ruff of longer white fur at jawline. Dark charcoal-grey stripes: crown pattern across forehead, radiating back over brows, down cheeks, banding neck. Pelt white to cream, not grey.

**EXPRESSION:** NOT sad. Determined, contained and dangerous — level brow, mouth set straight, jaw firm, chin level, direct unflinching gaze into or just past camera. Concentration and held anger, never sorrow or appeal.

**BUILD:** Lean and powerful, visible tendon and forearm, narrow waist, long limbs. Stands squared and still, weight settled, taking up full height, never hunching. Nothing rounded, nothing soft-cheeked, nothing childlike.

Patched canvas working coat, sleeves rolled short on forearms. Valve-key on loop at belt. Folded paper docket crushed in one fist.

**STYLE:** Flat graphic cel-shaded treatment, hard shadow shapes, crisp silhouette, matte surfaces — matching Corvain reference exactly. Not volumetric fur, not soft rounded volumes, not subsurface glow.

He stands at mouth of low brick outfall arch in sump lane. Hot white vent-fog pours around his knees, boils up past waist, goes opaque behind him. Shattered cast-iron Council notice board on wall to one side.

**Lighting:**
Hot sodium-yellow gaslight from behind and above, throwing hard orange rim along shoulders and top of head, firing through the fog. Cold blue-grey bounce off wet brick filling front of him. His white pelt is brightest value in frame.

**Palette:**
Saturated rust orange and sodium yellow against cold iron blue-grey, with bright steam white. The warmest and loudest of the three protagonists.

**Rarity/reference:**
Use Corvain as style reference. Epic rarity lighting (cinematic rim lighting and depth).

### LEGENDARY (2 cards)

#### THE DIFFERENTIAL ENGINE
*Legendary. Unaligned.*

**Subject:**
Not a machine you can see the whole of. Cathedral-scale chamber of brass and blackened iron. Cogwheels the size of houses turning slowly in dark. Thousands of thin rods and dial faces stacked to vanishing point overhead. Steam in long horizontal sheets. Catwalks and ladders on human scale near bottom of frame for contrast — tiny, almost missed.

At centre (where a face would be), great brass dial ring with open eye motif and needle that is not still.

Foreground: one small figure on walkway, back to us, dwarfed.

**Lighting:**
From deep inside the machine, upward and orange, like a furnace seen through a keyhole. The furnace glow is the only light source.

**Palette:**
Black, brass, furnace orange. Almost no cool tones. Frame should feel too small for the subject.

**Rarity signalling:**
Legendary — scale, atmosphere, sense of frame being too small. The Engine is LARGER than the frame can hold.

#### ALDOUS WROUGHT
*Legendary. Founder, deceased.*

**CRITICAL INSTRUCTION:**
This is NOT a painting in an old style. This is a stylised animation frame OF an old painting hung in a Council hall. Render the hall, the frame, the raking light across the canvas surface, the dust in the air. The PAINTING is an object in the world.

**Subject:**
Vast commemorative portrait, twelve feet tall in heavy gilt frame, century old and beginning to fail. Man in his sixties in high collar and long coat of a hundred years ago, hair swept back. One hand resting on brass orrery, other holding closed leather journal against chest. Pose is official myth: noble, forward-looking, slightly too perfect.

FAILURE IS THE POINT. Varnish yellowed and crazed. Water damage in lower corner. Eyes painted by someone who never met him. Behind him (painted as background allegory), eleven other figures in silhouette with unfinished faces — The Twelve.

Detail: the journal is the one thing rendered with sharp present-day clarity, as though it exists outside the painting.

**Lighting:**
Hard raking light from one high window across the canvas surface and into the Council hall around it. Dust visible in the beam.

**Palette:**
Aged varnish gold and cracked cream inside the frame; cool cream and tarnished silver in the hall around it.

**Rarity signalling:**
Legendary — scale and atmosphere. The painting dominates the composition; the frame and hall are secondary.

Cold grey light from high window fills the background ward and the edges of her form. The single warm source concentrates attention on her hands and the act of listening — the lamp light is the only warm thing in the scene.

**Palette:**
Grey-blue, iron, one clean warm circle of lamplight around hands. Rarity signalling (Uncommon) adds one strong source creating clear focus on the listening action.

### BOSS CARDS (10 cards)

Boss cards read as **encounters** rather than portraits: more environment, more threat, sense of viewer inside the scene rather than looking at a card. Portrait or landscape framing noted per card. All boss encounters share the same STYLE BLOCK and NEGATIVE BLOCK as other cards.

#### BOSS 1: THE FORCED RETAKE
*Chapter 1. Rooke. Scenario with named antagonist.*

**Faction block:** The Rise (Wrought) — the examining authority and institutional setting

**Framing:** Portrait (tall, vertical)

**Subject:**
Empty examination hall, six hundred desks in perfect rows, seen from perspective of one occupied desk. Sootfall on high windows. Scale is the threat.

**Vera Kross** (woman of fifty, black coat of senior examiner, iron-grey hair scraped into knot, face of straight lines, not cruel, utterly immovable) stands over the desk. She has just set a brass pocket watch down on the desk, face up, and taken her hand away.

Three other figures at distances: robed old man at distant lectern, grey-coated young officer at doors, second examiner standing apart with hands not steady.

**Threat reads as:** being seen accurately by somebody who cannot be lied to.

**Lighting:**
Cold institutional light from high windows above, falling down like a shaft on the one occupied desk where Rooke sits and Vera Kross stands over it. Warm brass glint off the pocket watch face and Vera's buttons. The scale of the hall (hundreds of desks receding) creates oppressive vastness; the light isolates the small area of confrontation in the centre.

**Palette:**
Grey-white, black coats, one small hard glint on watch glass. Rarity signalling (Boss) adds scale, atmosphere — sense that frame is too small for the entire hall.
