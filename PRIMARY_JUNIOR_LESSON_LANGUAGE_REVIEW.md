# Primary and Junior lesson language review

## Review decision

All 30 Primary lessons and all 33 visible Junior lessons have been reviewed and updated as one editorial pass. The pass covers 401 teaching sections and 1,604 worked examples.

The previous single four-part wrapper has been replaced by a judgement-based solution system:

- **Simple:** Think, Work, Answer and Check for direct counting, naming, reading and recognition. No formal problem-solving language is forced onto a question such as “How many digits are in 47?”
- **State → Work → Conclude:** the normal format for a calculation or a short chain of reasoning.
- **CLEAR:** Comprehend, Link the facts, Explain the route, Apply it carefully, Review and conclude. This is used for harder multi-step reasoning, proof, case analysis, optimisation and unfamiliar formula work.

The visible wording now explains “Comprehend” as “understand”. The Olympiad-style habits are used to make thinking visible, but the regular lesson examples remain separate from the Olympiad pathway.

## What changed

- Removed the abstract generic sequence that made easy questions harder to understand.
- Made simple examples short and concrete.
- Made the State section focus on the instruction to be answered rather than restating a whole topic method.
- Promoted full solutions that had accidentally been stored in an answer field into the visible worked steps.
- Made conclusions concise instead of repeating a complete solution.
- Removed unexplained truth-teller abbreviations from a difficult Junior logic example and divided its reasoning into shorter statements.
- Kept necessary mathematical words, such as numerator, denominator, gradient and hypotenuse, where the lesson teaches their meaning. Mathematical vocabulary is not replaced by vague everyday wording once it has been introduced.
- Added auditable solution-format data to every worked example and changed the lesson screen to render the chosen format.
- Replaced editorial stage names with the plain child-facing headings Example 1 to Example 4.
- Corrected the Junior Place Value comparison so it compares equal face values in different places: in 7.372, 7 ÷ 0.07 = 100. The earlier version incorrectly treated 8 and 7 as though their face values were equal.
- Made every State preserve the complete question. This prevents decimal points and other supplied facts from disappearing from the write-up.
- Removed generated section toppers such as “We start with...” and “This section grows from...” across all Primary and Junior lessons. They did not add mathematical meaning and one produced ungrammatical text.

## Acceptance evidence and remaining judgement

The lesson audit checks every visible topic, section and example. It now verifies the selected solution format, the complete State → Work → Conclude and CLEAR data, plain numbered example labels, full question preservation in State, correct treatment of multiplicative comparisons, structure links, four examples per section, non-repeated displayed questions and the absence of the abstract wrapper phrases that caused the Place Value failures. It currently reports zero review prompts.

That is evidence of coverage, not proof of comprehension. The next meaningful editorial test is to observe a child reading a small representative sample aloud and ask them to explain the method without help. Any sentence they cannot paraphrase should be treated as a rewrite prompt, even if every automated check passes.
